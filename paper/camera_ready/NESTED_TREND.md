## How to read these numbers

- An interval is the two-sided t interval on the per-seed means of a scope. The subset seeds are the replicates. The caches of a scope are fixed conditions that share one image set and one backbone, so no interval speaks for other corruptions.
- The per-K intervals are unadjusted and conditional on the image set: the seeds redraw rows inside one fixed set of images, and the reference is not conditional on it. A simulation of exactly valid per-class calibration put the standard deviation over image sets at 0.005 for K 10 and 0.008 for K 100, so a per-K interval that misses the reference by less than about 0.02 is not read as evidence.
- The oracle sits on the reference by construction: a random pool and evaluation split of a class's rows is exchangeable whatever the shift.
- Split, weighted, clustered and ACP calibrate one threshold across classes and target marginal coverage, so they need not sit on a per-class reference even without shift.
- The moderate five caches were chosen from realised outcomes of the earlier independent run. Their scope is descriptive.
- Write a for the worst-class value at K 10, b for the minimum over the K 10 classes of their coverage inside the K 100 problem, and c for the worst-class value at K 100. The selection term is pinned at minus the reference's decline when b is 0 or c is 1, whatever the method does, and the same-class term is one-sided when a is 0 or 1. A term marked bound has at least 0.50 of its cells in that state and is not to be interpreted. With a smaller share the mean over the other cells is read beside the term, and an exclusion of zero is not reported when that mean has the other sign.
- Every cache of a scope shares one class order per seed, which skews the per-seed means. The skewness is printed beside the intervals, and the selection term is repeated with b replaced by its expectation over a random K 10 subset. A family exclusion that this version does not reproduce is flagged and not reported as evidence.
- The no-shift terms are the yardstick for the methods that target marginal coverage: a negative term of such a method under shift is a shift effect only as far as it goes beyond the same term without shift.
- Clustered is outside the family. It draws its internal split over row positions with its own seed fixed at 0. At one K the number of calibration points each position of the class layout keeps is therefore the same for every subset seed, while the rows in those positions change with the seed. Between K 10 and K 100 the mask, the share set aside for clustering and a class's position all change, so its same-class term compares calibration sets that are not the same rows, and at K 10 its rule skips clustering.
- Weighted fits its domain classifier on the K kept logits, so the classifier changes with K together with the label space, and its mean set size over K changes with it. Its same-class term mixes the two.
- At K 100 every seed holds all 100 classes, so the seeds vary only the calibration subsample and the pool and evaluation split.
