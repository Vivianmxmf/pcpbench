## How to read these numbers

- An interval is the two-sided t interval on the per-seed means of a scope. The subset seeds are the replicates. The caches of a scope are fixed conditions that share one image set and one backbone, so no interval speaks for other corruptions.
- The per-K intervals are unadjusted and conditional on the image set: the seeds redraw rows inside one fixed set of images, and the reference is not conditional on it. A simulation of exactly valid per-class calibration put the standard deviation over image sets at 0.005 for K 10 and 0.008 for K 100, so a per-K interval that misses the reference by less than about 0.02 is not read as evidence.
- The oracle sits on the reference by construction: a random pool and evaluation split of a class's rows is exchangeable whatever the shift.
- Split, weighted, clustered and ACP calibrate one threshold across classes and target marginal coverage, so they need not sit on a per-class reference even without shift.
- The moderate five caches were chosen from realised outcomes of the earlier independent run. Their scope is descriptive.
